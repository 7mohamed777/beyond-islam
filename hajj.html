🕋 Hajj (Pilgrimage) in Islam - The Fifth Pillar

Hajj is the fifth pillar of Islam, an annual pilgrimage to Mecca that every adult Muslim who is physically and financially able must perform at least once in their lifetime. It is the largest gathering of humanity for worship, uniting Muslims from all corners of the world in a profound demonstration of equality, submission, and devotion to Allah.

🕋 Fifth Pillar Obligatory once in a lifetime
🌍 Global Gathering Millions unite annually
🤲 Ultimate Submission Complete devotion to Allah

📖 The Divine Command in the Quran

Hajj traces its origins to Prophet Ibrahim (A.S) who was commanded by Allah to build the Kaaba and call humanity to pilgrimage. The obligation is clearly established in the Quran.

Surah Al-Imran 3:97
وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا ۚ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَنِيٌّ عَنِ الْعَالَمِينَ
"And due to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House - for whoever is able to find thereto a way. But whoever disbelieves - then indeed, Allah is free from need of the worlds."
Verse 97
Surah Al-Hajj 22:27
وَأَذِّن فِي النَّاسِ بِالْحَجِّ يَأْتُوكَ رِجَالًا وَعَلَىٰ كُلِّ ضَامِرٍ يَأْتِينَ مِن كُلِّ فَجٍّ عَمِيقٍ
"And proclaim to the people the Hajj; they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel; they will come from every distant pass."
Verse 27
Historical Connection:

Hajj connects Muslims directly with Prophet Ibrahim (A.S), Prophet Ismail (A.S), and Hajar (A.S) - reenacting their supreme acts of faith, sacrifice, and submission that established the sacred sites of Mecca.

📋 The Three Types of Hajj

Islam gives pilgrims flexibility in how they combine or separate the rituals of Hajj and Umrah. Scholars unanimously recognize three valid forms of Hajj, each with its own steps, requirements, and scholarly opinions on merit. Understanding each type helps every pilgrim make an informed, correct intention before entering Ihram — because once the intention is made, changing it has conditions and consequences.

1. Hajj al-Ifrad (إفراد) — Hajj Alone

What it means: The word Ifrad comes from the Arabic root meaning "to single out" or "to isolate." The pilgrim enters Ihram exclusively for Hajj, with no Umrah connected to it during the same trip.

How it works step by step:

  • The pilgrim makes the intention: "Labbayk Allahumma Hajjan" (Here I am, O Allah, for Hajj) at the Miqat.
  • He or she remains in Ihram continuously from the Miqat all the way through the completion of all Hajj rituals — Wuquf at Arafat, Muzdalifah, Mina, Tawaf al-Ifadah, Sa'i, and shaving or trimming.
  • No Umrah is performed as part of this Hajj trip.
  • Because Hajj and Umrah are not combined, no sacrificial animal (Hady) is required as an obligation — though offering one voluntarily is recommended.

Who typically performs Ifrad? Residents of Mecca and the surrounding area (within the Miqat boundaries) are encouraged toward Ifrad, as Umrah is easily accessible to them year-round. It is also chosen by those who have already performed Umrah earlier in the same year, or those who wish to focus their worship entirely on Hajj without the additional steps of Umrah.

Scholarly View on Ifrad
Imam al-Shafi'i and Imam Malik considered Ifrad the most virtuous form of Hajj. They reasoned that keeping the acts of worship pure and separated gives each one its full weight and focus. This view is supported by narrations from Aisha (R.A) indicating that the Prophet (S.A.W) entered Ihram for Hajj alone on his Farewell Pilgrimage.

2. Hajj al-Qiran (قِران) — Combining Umrah and Hajj in One Ihram

What it means: The word Qiran means "joining" or "pairing." The pilgrim enters Ihram for both Umrah and Hajj simultaneously, performing both under a single unbroken state of Ihram — from the Miqat all the way to the final day of Hajj.

How it works step by step:

  • At the Miqat, the pilgrim makes a combined intention: "Labbayk Allahumma Umratan wa Hajjan" (Here I am, O Allah, for both Umrah and Hajj together).
  • Upon arriving in Mecca, the pilgrim performs Tawaf al-Qudum (the arrival Tawaf) and the Sa'i between Safa and Marwa.
  • Crucially, the pilgrim does not exit Ihram after completing Umrah. All Ihram restrictions remain in force without interruption until the full completion of Hajj rituals on the 10th of Dhul Hijjah (shaving or trimming after the sacrifice).
  • The Tawaf and Sa'i performed for Umrah upon arrival also count for Hajj, so a second full Sa'i after Tawaf al-Ifadah is not strictly required according to most scholars, though some recommend it.
  • A sacrificial animal (Hady) is obligatory for the person performing Qiran, as established in Surah Al-Baqarah 2:196.

The longest Ihram: Qiran involves the longest continuous period in the state of Ihram — potentially several weeks, from arriving at the Miqat all the way until the 10th of Dhul Hijjah. This demands extraordinary patience, especially in avoiding all Ihram prohibitions (no perfume, no stitched garments for men, no cutting of hair or nails, no marital relations) for such an extended time.

Who performs Qiran? Historically, Qiran was performed by those who brought their sacrificial animal (Hady) with them from home, since once the animal is designated and brought, the pilgrim traditionally remains in Ihram until it is slaughtered. Today it is less common but still valid and rewarded.

Scholarly View on Qiran
Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal considered Qiran the most virtuous of the three forms, citing multiple hadith indicating that the Prophet (S.A.W) himself performed Qiran on his Farewell Pilgrimage and brought 100 sacrificial camels. The unbroken devotion from start to finish is seen as the highest expression of complete submission.

3. Hajj al-Tamattu' (تَمَتُّع) — The Most Common Form Today

What it means: The word Tamattu' means "to enjoy" or "to benefit." The pilgrim performs Umrah first, fully exits Ihram after completing it, and enjoys a period of normal daily life in Mecca before re-entering Ihram specifically for Hajj on the 8th of Dhul Hijjah. It is called Tamattu' because the pilgrim "enjoys" the relief of exiting Ihram between the two acts of worship.

How it works step by step:

  • Step 1 — Umrah Ihram: Upon reaching the Miqat during the Hajj months (Shawwal, Dhul Qa'dah, or Dhul Hijjah), the pilgrim makes the intention for Umrah only: "Labbayk Allahumma Umratan".
  • Step 2 — Umrah rituals in Mecca: The pilgrim performs Tawaf al-Umrah (7 circuits around the Kaaba), followed by Sa'i (7 walks between Safa and Marwa), and then shaves the head or trims the hair.
  • Step 3 — Full exit from Ihram: After Umrah is complete, the pilgrim exits Ihram entirely. All restrictions are lifted — regular clothes, perfume, food and drink without restriction, and marital relations are all permitted again.
  • Step 4 — The interval in Mecca: The pilgrim remains in Mecca in a normal state until the 8th of Dhul Hijjah. This period — which can be several days to over a week depending on arrival date — is an ideal time for rest, additional worship, visiting the Haram, and mental preparation for the intense days of Hajj ahead.
  • Step 5 — Re-entering Ihram for Hajj: On the morning of the 8th of Dhul Hijjah (Yawm al-Tarwiyah), the pilgrim puts on Ihram again — this time with the intention for Hajj alone: "Labbayk Allahumma Hajjan". This Ihram is entered from wherever the pilgrim is staying in Mecca.
  • Step 6 — Full Hajj journey begins: From this point, the complete Hajj sequence begins: travel to Mina, standing at Arafat on the 9th, night at Muzdalifah, stoning the Jamarat, sacrifice, shaving, Tawaf al-Ifadah, Sa'i, and finally Tawaf al-Wada' before departure.
  • Sacrifice (Hady) is obligatory for Tamattu'. Those who genuinely cannot afford it must fast 3 days during Hajj (before Eid al-Adha) and 7 days after returning home — a total of 10 days — as specified in Surah Al-Baqarah 2:196.

Why is Tamattu' recommended for most pilgrims worldwide? The Prophet (S.A.W) explicitly said to his companions: "If I were to start over, I would not have brought the sacrificial animal and would have made it Tamattu'." (Bukhari & Muslim). The relief of exiting Ihram between Umrah and Hajj gives the pilgrim physical and mental recovery time — essential when spending weeks in a hot, crowded environment far from home. For modern pilgrims who arrive early in Dhul Hijjah, this break can last over a week, allowing thorough rest and spiritual preparation before the most intense days of worship in the Islamic calendar.

Scholarly View on Tamattu'
Imam Abu Hanifa considered Tamattu' the most virtuous form. The majority of contemporary scholars, fatwa councils, and Hajj ministries worldwide recommend Tamattu' for pilgrims traveling from outside Saudi Arabia. Its combination of prophetic endorsement, spiritual depth across two separate acts of worship, and practical relief for long-distance travelers makes it the predominant choice for millions of pilgrims every year.

🕋 The Prophet's Final Guidance:

The Prophet (S.A.W) performed Qiran on his Farewell Pilgrimage because he had brought his sacrificial animals with him from Madinah. Yet he said to his companions who had not brought animals: "If I were to do it over, I would not have brought the sacrificial animal and would have made it Tamattu'." This profound statement shows the Prophet's compassion — he wanted ease for his Ummah. Whatever type you choose, the key is to learn it completely, make the intention with full awareness, and perform every ritual with sincerity and presence of heart. The reward is ultimately with Allah.

🏛️ Sacred Sites of Hajj

🕋 Masjid al-Haram & The Kaaba

The holiest site in Islam, containing the Kaaba - the "House of Allah" built by Ibrahim and Ismail. Muslims worldwide face this direction in prayer. The Black Stone (Hajar al-Aswad) is set in the eastern corner.

🏃 Safa and Marwa

Two small hills now enclosed within the Grand Mosque. Pilgrims perform Sa'i (walking seven times between them) commemorating Hajar's desperate search for water for her infant son Ismail.

⛰️ Mount Arafat

The most crucial site of Hajj - "Hajj is Arafah." Pilgrims gather here on the 9th of Dhul Hijjah for the day of standing (Wuquf), making supplication and seeking forgiveness. Without standing at Arafat, Hajj is invalid.

🛏️ Muzdalifah

An open area between Arafat and Mina where pilgrims spend the night after Arafat, collecting pebbles for the stoning ritual and sleeping under the open sky.

🏕️ Mina (City of Tents)

A vast tent city where pilgrims spend several days, performing the stoning of the three pillars (Jamarat) commemorating Ibrahim's stoning of Satan when he tried to dissuade him from sacrificing his son.

📅 Hajj Step by Step: The Journey of a Lifetime

Day 1 8th Dhul Hijjah - Tarwiyah Day
1
Enter Ihram
Pilgrims enter the state of Ihram at the Miqat (designated station), making intention and reciting Talbiyah: "Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk..."
2
Travel to Mina
Pilgrims proceed to Mina and spend the day and night in prayer and reflection.
Day 2 9th Dhul Hijjah - Day of Arafah (The Greatest Day)
3
Depart to Arafat
After sunrise, pilgrims leave Mina for the plain of Arafat.
4
Wuquf - Standing at Arafat
The essential ritual of Hajj. Pilgrims combine Dhuhr and Asr prayers and spend the day in supplication, Quran recitation, and seeking forgiveness until sunset.
5
Muzdalifah
After sunset, pilgrims proceed to Muzdalifah, combine Maghrib and Isha prayers, collect 49-70 pebbles for Jamarat, and spend the night under the open sky.
Day 3 10th Dhul Hijjah - Eid al-Adha (Day of Sacrifice)
6
Stoning the Jamarat al-Aqabah
Before sunrise, proceed to Mina and throw 7 pebbles at the largest pillar (Jamarat al-Aqabah) saying "Allahu Akbar" with each throw.
7
Sacrifice (Hady)
Offer a sacrificial animal (sheep, goat, cow, or camel) commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son.
8
Shave or Trim Hair (Halq or Taqsir)
Men shave their heads or trim hair; women cut a fingertip's length. This partially exits Ihram, permitting everything except marital relations.
9
Tawaf al-Ifadah
Proceed to Mecca, perform Tawaf (7 circuits around Kaaba) and Sa'i (7 walks between Safa and Marwa). This completes the major rituals, allowing full exit from Ihram.
10
Return to Mina
Spend the night of the 10th and 11th in Mina.
Days 4-5 11th-12th Dhul Hijjah - Days of Tashreeq
11
Stoning All Three Jamarat
Each day after Dhuhr, throw 7 pebbles at each pillar: first the small (Ula), then middle (Wusta), then large (Aqabah), making 21 pebbles daily.
12
Stay in Mina
Remain in Mina for worship and reflection.
Day 6 13th Dhul Hijjah - Optional Extension
13
Final Stoning
If still in Mina, stone all three Jamarat again after Dhuhr. Most pilgrims depart on 12th.
Final Before Departure
14
Tawaf al-Wada (Farewell Tawaf)
Before leaving Mecca, perform a final Tawaf of seven circuits as farewell. This is the last ritual before departing the holy city.
The Prophet's Description
"Learn your rituals from me." (Muslim) - The Prophet (S.A.W) demonstrated every ritual, saying this as he performed Hajj.

👕 Ihram: The Garment of Equality

👕 Men's Ihram Two white seamless sheets
👗 Women's Ihram Modest clothing, face uncovered
🤝 Symbol of Equality All dressed alike before Allah
Prohibitions in Ihram:

While in the state of Ihram, pilgrims must avoid: cutting hair/nails, using perfume, hunting, marital relations, arguing, fighting, and (for men) wearing stitched clothing or covering head.

The Talbiyah - Pilgrim's Chant
لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ
"Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk, Labbayka la sharika laka Labbayk, Innal hamda wan ni'mata laka wal mulk, la sharika lak"
(Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. Indeed, all praise and blessings are Yours, and all sovereignty. You have no partner.)

"That is so. And whoever honors the symbols of Allah - indeed, it is from the piety of hearts."
Verse 32
🌍 Global Gathering One humanity, one Creator
🔄 Cycle of Return Leaving everything for Allah
🌟 Transformation Coming home spiritually reborn

Hajj is not merely a journey to Mecca - it is a journey to Allah. It is leaving behind the comforts of home, the distinctions of wealth and status, and the distractions of the world to stand before the Creator with nothing but one's deeds and sincere repentance. In the white garments of Ihram, all are equal - kings and servants, rich and poor, Arab and non-Arab - all responding to the call of Ibrahim that echoes through millennia: "Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk" (Here I am, O Allah, here I am). The pilgrim returns not just with souvenirs, but with a renewed soul, sins forgiven, and the hope of Paradise - the ultimate destination to which Hajj itself is but a preview.
